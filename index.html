Communicable Disease Review

Malaria

"Parasitic disease – transmitted by Anopheles mosquito. Preventable, treatable."

Epidemiology & Overview

Malaria – protozoan disease caused by Plasmodium species, transmitted by female Anopheles mosquito.

Public Health Significance (Philippines)

  • Endemic in many provinces; highest incidence in Palawan, Mindoro, Mindanao (Regions 9, 10, 12, CARAGA, Bangsamoro).
  • Annual parasite incidence (API) target: reduction by 20% annually (1994 goal).
  • Major vector: Anopheles flavirostris (bites at night, prefers clean water).
  • Four species: P. falciparum (most severe), P. vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae.

Agent & Life Cycle

Infective stage to humans: Sporozoites (in mosquito salivary glands).

Parasite development: Sporozoites → liver (exoerythrocytic) → merozoites → invade RBCs → asexual cycle → gametocytes → mosquito.

Incubation period: 10‑35 days (depending on species).

Clinical Features & Diagnosis

Classic Paroxysms (P. vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae)

  • Cold stage: sudden onset of chills, rigors (15‑60 min).
  • Hot stage: high fever (40‑41°C), headache, vomiting, flushing (2‑6 hours).
  • Sweating stage: profuse sweating, temperature drops, exhaustion (2‑4 hours).
  • Cyclical fever: P. vivax/ovale – every 48h (tertian); P. malariae – every 72h (quartan).
  • P. falciparum: irregular fever, may cause severe complications (cerebral malaria, ARDS, renal failure).

Diagnosis

  • Microscopy: Thick and thin blood smear – gold standard; identifies species and parasitemia.
  • Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT): detects HRP2/pLDH; useful in remote areas.
  • PCR: for species confirmation and research.
  • Clinical: fever + history of travel to endemic area + chills.

Transmission & Vector

Mode of Transmission

  • Bite of female Anopheles mosquito (usually bites from dusk to dawn).
  • Blood transfusion (rare), congenital (from mother to fetus), needle‑sharing (IV drug users).
  • NOT directly person‑to‑person.

Vector Characteristics & Control

  • Anopheles flavirostris – primary vector in Philippines; breeds in clean, slow‑moving water (streams, rice paddies).
  • Peak biting: 9 PM – 3 AM.
  • Resting habits: indoor (endophilic) and outdoor.

Prevention & Control

Personal Protective Measures

  • Insecticide‑treated mosquito nets (ITNs) – most effective.
  • Wear long sleeves, long pants, socks (especially evening).
  • Use insect repellents (DEET, picaridin).
  • Screen windows and doors.
  • Chemoprophylaxis: Chloroquine (for sensitive areas) or doxycycline/mefloquine for travelers to endemic zones.

Vector Control Measures

  • Indoor residual spraying (IRS) with insecticides.
  • Environmental management: drainage, filling breeding sites, stream clearing.
  • Larvivorous fish (e.g., Gambusia) in rice fields and streams.
  • Zooprophylaxis: keeping animals (carabaos) near houses to divert mosquitoes.

Treatment & Complications

Antimalarial Therapy (DOH guidelines)

  • Uncomplicated P. falciparum: Artemisinin‑based combination therapy (ACT) – e.g., artemether‑lumefantrine.
  • P. vivax / P. ovale: Chloroquine + Primaquine (to kill hypnozoites and prevent relapse).
  • Severe malaria (IV): Artesunate IV (preferred) or quinine.
  • Chemoprophylaxis: Chloroquine weekly (for areas without resistance) or doxycycline daily.

Complications (Severe Falciparum)

  • Cerebral malaria (coma, seizures)
  • Acute kidney injury (blackwater fever)
  • ARDS, pulmonary edema
  • Severe anemia, thrombocytopenia
  • Hypoglycemia, metabolic acidosis
  • Death if untreated

Drug Resistance & Monitoring

  • Chloroquine resistance – widespread in P. falciparum.
  • ACT remains effective; monitor for artemisinin resistance.
  • Primaquine contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (hemolysis).

PHN Role & Nursing Care

  • Case finding & diagnosis: Recognize fever patterns; collect blood smear (thick/thin) – send to microscopy.
  • Treatment support: Ensure compliance with ACT; supervise directly if possible (DOT for malaria).
  • Health education: Use of insecticide‑treated nets, repellents, clearing breeding sites.
  • Vector control: Coordinate with local government for stream clearing, larvivorous fish, indoor spraying.
  • Chemoprophylaxis advice: For pregnant women (intermittent preventive treatment – IPT) using sulfadoxine‑pyrimethamine.
  • Monitoring: Watch for signs of severe malaria (altered consciousness, oliguria, bleeding). Refer immediately.
  • Surveillance: Malaria is notifiable; submit weekly reports (FHSIS/M‑1).
  • Outbreak response: Mass blood smear, focal spraying, distribution of ITNs.

Related DOH Programs:

Malaria Control Service · Roll Back Malaria · FHSIS (Monthly Report of Malaria) · Insecticide‑treated net distribution
